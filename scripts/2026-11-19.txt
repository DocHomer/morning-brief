Turning to world news.

President Trump is in Shenzhen, China, for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. Today he met one-on-one with Chinese President Xi Jinping, then joined Xi and Russian President Vladimir Putin for the first three-way meeting of the three leaders. The Associated Press reports nuclear arms and the wars in Ukraine and Iran were on the agenda. President Trump said the three countries are, quote, uniquely positioned, to work on reducing nuclear weapons. Xi said the talks would focus on promoting world peace, and Putin pointed to the three nations' shared history as allies in World War Two. In the one-on-one meeting, Xi called U.S.-China relations generally stable and improved. China's Xinhua news agency reported that Xi raised Taiwan, and President Trump said he would press China to buy more American farm goods. The New START arms control treaty between the U.S. and Russia expired in February. China is estimated to have more than 600 nuclear warheads, compared with more than 5,000 each for the U.S. and Russia. Nine Democratic senators, led by Jeanne Shaheen of New Hampshire, wrote to the president urging him to press Xi on China's support for Russia. They wrote that American security, quote, cannot be traded away for photo opportunities. No agreements from the three-way meeting had been announced as of this morning.

Before that meeting, President Trump and Putin talked for about 40 minutes, according to Radio Free Europe. U.S. officials called it a pull-aside. It was their first face-to-face meeting since the Alaska summit in 2025. The president called Putin his friend and said, quote, we'll have a second meeting later on. Reporters were not allowed in. Ukraine's top diplomat in Washington, Denys Sienik, said issues involving Ukraine must include Ukraine.

Before arriving in China, the president stopped in Manila, where Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Junior welcomed him with a 21-gun salute. The two discussed energy and defense, and Marcos named technology and artificial intelligence as new areas for cooperation. The president announced what he called a very big trade deal, and the White House released agreements with the Philippines and Thailand.

Republicans Josh Hawley and Mike Rounds voted no, along with 29 Democrats and members of their caucus. Supporters include industry groups and major unions. Environmental groups and tribes oppose it. Turning to Kentucky.

Republican legislative leaders say they are considering reopening the state's two-year budget to limit Governor Andy Beshear's ability to spend surplus money. A minister has asked Governor Beshear to commute the sentences of all 23 people on Kentucky's death row. The Lexington Herald-Leader reports the Reverend Jeff Hood, a former Louisville resident, sent the letter Monday. Kentucky has not carried out an execution since 2008. Attorney General Russell Coleman has pushed to resume them. A spokesperson for the governor said every pardon request is considered on the individual facts of the case. Now to artificial intelligence.

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude chatbot, priced its initial public offering at 83 dollars a share, above its expected range of 70 to 78 dollars. The AP reports that values the company at about 1.5 trillion dollars and raises about 81 billion dollars, which would be the largest stock offering ever, ahead of SpaceX in June. Shares are set to begin trading today on the Nasdaq under the ticker A N T. The AP also reports Anthropic is not profitable under standard accounting rules, carries 6.2 billion dollars in debt, and has committed about 900 billion dollars to computing and infrastructure in coming years. Anthropic is a public benefit corporation, a legal structure that requires it to weigh a public mission alongside profit.

A report from the Government Accountability Office, the nonpartisan investigative arm of Congress, found that investigators got four major chatbots to produce information that could help plan attacks. The chatbots were Anthropic's Claude, xAI's Grok, OpenAI's ChatGPT and Google's Gemini. Investigators used well-known jailbreak techniques, which are ways of tricking a model into ignoring its safety rules, and got results within 12 hours. ABC News reports two platforms produced bombing plans for major U.S. cities, and two gave instructions for targeting electrical substations. The report did not say which chatbot produced which result. All four companies told the G A O they have since improved their safeguards, and OpenAI said the testing used an older version of ChatGPT. Democratic Senator Maggie Hassan of New Hampshire requested the study and plans hearings and legislation next year.

And Google released a new mid-priced model called Gemini 4 Neon, which it says is built for software engineering, office work and science. Google says it can track down bugs across entire code projects and edit several files in fewer steps. For developers, it costs 3 dollars per million tokens of input. A token is a small piece of text, about three-quarters of a word. It's also available to paying Google A I Pro and Ultra subscribers.
