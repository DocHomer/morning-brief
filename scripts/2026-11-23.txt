Turning to world news.

The United Nations climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, ended Sunday, two days late, without an overall agreement. CBS News reports talks broke down over whether to name fossil fuels. An early draft called for accelerated action to phase them out, but that language was removed. According to The Conversation, Saudi Arabia and Russia blocked references to fossil fuels, and then the European Union, Panama and several island nations rejected the compromise text offered by hosts Turkey and Australia. Panama's environment minister, Juan Carlos Navarro, said, quote, we cannot continue to be held hostage by petroleum producers. Australia's climate minister, Chris Bowen, said the talks hit, quote, unbridgeable red lines. U.N. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres blamed, quote, powerful fossil fuel interests. Outside the formal talks, more than 75 countries joined a pledge on electrification, and Brazil launched a road map for phasing out fossil fuels. The United States did not send a delegation. Next year's summit is in Ethiopia.

In Ukraine, the death toll from Sunday's Russian drone strike on an apartment building in Dymer, north of Kyiv, has risen to 11. The head of the Kyiv regional administration, Tymur Tkachenko, announced the new toll this morning after another body was recovered. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said part of the five-story building collapsed. Ukraine's emergency service said crews worked through the night, pausing only for air raid alerts, and pulled a seven-year-old boy named Davyd from the rubble alive after about 14 hours. The service called it, quote, a true miracle. A separate drone strike on a grocery store in Vyshneve, southwest of Kyiv, killed three people, according to the Ukrinform news agency. On diplomacy, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said today that the next round of talks among Russia, the United States and Ukraine is expected soon, and, quote, for now, we are working on the assumption of Abu Dhabi. No date has been set.

NATO ambassadors meet in Brussels today to discuss the Russian missiles that came down in eastern Poland on Friday. Polish prosecutors said Sunday that debris found at three sites came from Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles, and initial findings indicate they were made within recent weeks. No one was hurt. Stars and Stripes reports one missile landed about 22 miles from a town where thousands of European troops were holding an exercise. Polish Defense Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz said, quote, it is hard to regard it as a coincidence, and said Poland expects strong support from NATO allies to protect its eastern border. Poland has asked for more NATO surveillance aircraft and F-35 fighter jets.

In the Persian Gulf, Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it struck U.S. military positions in Bahrain late Saturday with missiles and drones, saying it was retaliating for U.S. drone flights over Iran. Bahrain's Interior Ministry said attacks were intercepted, and a fire broke out at a civilian industrial site, with material damage. Reports on injuries differ. Bahrain asked the U.N. Security Council to take, quote, firm measures against Iran, and Jordan called the attack a dangerous escalation. The United States and Israel have been at war with Iran since late February. The outlet Iran International reports President Trump is weighing whether to resume strikes, and that he said he is inclined to do so after the new year if there is no deal. An Iranian deputy foreign minister said, quote, the window of diplomacy is still open.

And in Sudan, drone attacks blamed on the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces killed at least four people in and around Khartoum early Sunday, according to Al Jazeera. A U.N. building was damaged while staff were inside. The U.N. humanitarian office called the risk to its personnel unacceptable.

Now to news here in the U.S.

Democrats have clinched their largest House majority since 1992. Republican Congresswoman Virginia Foxx of North Carolina, an 11-term incumbent and chair of the House Rules Committee, conceded Sunday to Democrat Chuck Hubbard after a recount. Hubbard won by 330 votes out of about 287,000. Foxx said she was, quote, saddened that I did not emerge victorious, and congratulated Hubbard. Democrats now hold at least 258 House seats, a gain of about 47, with four races still uncalled. Democratic Congresswoman Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez called it a, quote, mandate for change. The new Congress is sworn in January 3rd, with Hakeem Jeffries in line to become speaker.

Senate Democrats will pick their next leader by secret ballot on December 1st, with incumbent Chuck Schumer of New York facing Chris Van Hollen of Maryland. The Associated Press reports Senator Elissa Slotkin of Michigan and several incoming senators back Van Hollen, while Senators Sherrod Brown, Mazie Hirono and Ruben Gallego back Schumer. Gallego said, quote, when the manager takes you to the World Series, you give him another contract. Van Hollen is promising a more confrontational approach toward President Trump.

A full federal appeals court has ruled that the House's pandemic-era proxy voting was unconstitutional. The Fifth Circuit, based in New Orleans, ruled in a case brought by Texas over the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act, which passed as part of a 1.7 trillion dollar spending package in late 2022. Bloomberg Law reports Judge Cory Wilson wrote that members of Congress, quote, must be present for quorum purposes, though he limited the ruling to that one law. Judge James Graves dissented, questioning why relief was limited to, quote, one small sliver of the bill. An appeal to the Supreme Court is expected.

The F B I is investigating an apparent breach of the National Crime Information Center, the database police across the country use for warrants and background checks. CNN first reported it, and it is the bureau's second reported breach since September. Separately, NBC News reported the Agriculture Department told employees a system used to pay farmers was hit by a cyberattack. It is not clear whether the incidents are connected. President Trump said he only recently learned of the F B I breach, that he did not like it, and urged the public to relax.

On Wall Street, investors start a holiday-shortened week after the market's worst week since 2020. TheStreet reports stock futures rose over the weekend. This week brings consumer confidence numbers Tuesday, then a heavy Wednesday with jobless claims, the Fed's preferred inflation measure, and economic growth figures. Markets are closed Thursday and close early Friday. As of last Thursday, futures traders put the odds of a Federal Reserve rate increase in December at more than 97 percent, according to the CME Group.

And Thanksgiving travel will run into weather on both coasts. In California, a coastal flood warning for the San Francisco Bay shoreline runs from this morning through Thursday afternoon, with the highest tides in 18 years coinciding with an atmospheric river, a long plume of moisture off the Pacific. San Francisco Mayor Daniel Lurie said, quote, we are anticipating high tides and rain that this city has never seen before. In the East, the Fox Forecast Center says a coastal storm forming off Florida could stretch to Maine and affect more than 20 states, with the worst on Thanksgiving Day. AAA projects 81.5 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles this week, about the same as last year, with Tuesday and Wednesday expected to be the busiest days on the roads.

Now, Kentucky news.

New federal limits on hemp products take effect December 11th. Finished hemp-derived products will be capped at four-tenths of a milligram of THC per container, and anything above that would no longer count as legal hemp. WKYT reports the owner of a Lexington hemp shop, Annie Rouse of Cannabuzz, estimates only about 2 percent of her products would comply, and said, quote, we are having to cut back workforce and production. Senator Mitch McConnell helped write the restrictions into the bill that ended last year's government shutdown, and his spokesperson said he opposes another delay. Rouse said she hopes the new Congress revisits the issue.

In Fleming County, an 8-year-old boy is in critical condition after falling out of an enclosed box trailer onto Kentucky 32 on Saturday. Kentucky State Police say two adults, 47-year-old Marvin Hitch and 42-year-old Viola Hitch, were moving furniture with the boy inside the trailer when he leaned on the door, it opened, and he fell into the road. He was flown to the children's hospital at UK HealthCare in Lexington. Each adult is charged with six counts of first-degree wanton endangerment and one count of second-degree criminal abuse.

In Frankfort, Governor Andy Beshear spoke at the Salvation Army's Christmas campaign kickoff and said 80,000 Kentuckians have less access to food this year than last year, including almost 25,000 children and more than 6,500 seniors. Beshear tied the strain to cuts in SNAP food assistance and to federal tariffs. The Red Kettle and Angel Tree campaigns are now underway statewide.

In Woodford County, one person was killed Sunday evening in a crash between a car and a pickup truck on the eastbound Bluegrass Parkway in Versailles. The coroner has not released the driver's name, and police are investigating the cause.

Police allege he drove two other suspects, who have not been identified, to a home on South 45th Street, where Carr was shot multiple times. A 12-year-old girl and a woman inside the home were also hit. Police say they traced a stolen car through video. Bruin is charged with murder, wanton endangerment and receiving stolen property. Now, AI news.

Anthropic, which went public on the Nasdaq last week in what Al Jazeera called the biggest stock offering of all time, is drawing attention for how it handled the deal. According to Reuters, Anthropic barred some of its I P O bankers from working with rivals, including OpenAI, for six months, and limited each bank's deal team to four senior bankers. Anthropic's stock fell about 9 percent in Friday's selloff, along with much of the technology sector.

Pressure is building in Washington over Nvidia's chips reaching China. Fox News reports the Director of National Intelligence said Sunday he expects China has found ways to obtain Nvidia's most advanced chips, pointing to a federal indictment in New York. The chair of the House Intelligence Committee said Nvidia leadership is expected at a closed-door interview with the committee on December 1st, and lawmakers are weighing legislation on chip sales to China. Nvidia has been led by an interim chief executive, Colette Kress, since Jensen Huang resigned earlier this month. Also Sunday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the AI industry, quote, has done a poor job explaining itself on data centers, and blamed last week's market drop partly on Democratic opposition to data centers and on the Federal Reserve.

Together AI, a company that runs AI models for businesses, disclosed that customer prompts were exposed after one of its own AI coding agents, software that writes code automatically, accidentally published a private password-like credential to a public code site. The company says an unauthorized party used it to download stored samples of customer requests from June and July. Together says new accounts will now have their data not retained by default.

And in mathematics, an Anthropic researcher, Levent Alpoge, says that working with the company's Claude AI, he has found a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, a famous unsolved problem about prime numbers that dates to 1859 and carries a million dollar prize. The claim has not been verified. The proof was released as roughly 17 million lines of computer-checkable code, according to Kevin Buzzard of Imperial College London, who said, quote, at the minute, we don't even know why the theorem is true. Buzzard said he expects mathematicians to understand the code within a week or two.
