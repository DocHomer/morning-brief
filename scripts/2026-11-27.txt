Good morning, this is The Morning Brief for Friday, November 27. Today: Kuwait says an Iranian drone attack killed two Italian workers, storms knock out power to hundreds of thousands of homes and businesses over Thanksgiving, the Bills beat the Chiefs on a 57-yard field goal as time expired, Louisville beats Rick Pitino and St. John's at the buzzer, and the Bengals hold their first full practice of the week for the Saints.

Turning to world news.

Kuwait's Defense Ministry says Iranian drones targeted what it called a vital site in the country on Thursday. The ministry said the drones were intercepted, but falling debris injured four people, one Kuwaiti, one Pakistani and two Italians. The two Italians, both technicians, later died. Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani told the Italian Senate the drones hit around 2 p.m. local time, including the site where the two men worked, and said, quote, there was a powerful explosion. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said, quote, we must step up pressure on Iran. Iran denies it. An Iranian armed forces spokesman told the state news agency IRNA that, quote, no military or offensive operations have been conducted, and called the accusation a false flag. Iran's embassy in Rome also rejected the claim.

The fighting between the Houthis in Yemen and Saudi Arabia continued. The Saudi Press Agency, citing the Energy Ministry, says a strike on the Jazan refinery on Thursday killed a Nepali worker, injured several others, started a fire and forced a temporary shutdown. The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed the ballistic missile launcher used in the attack. Saudi air defenses also intercepted two ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh and two drones over the Eastern Province. The Houthis say they attacked the Ras Tanura refinery on Wednesday. The coalition says it intercepted four drones there and did not say the facility was hit. The United Nations relief chief, Tom Fletcher, visiting Yemen, said half the country's population needs aid, and urged the Houthis to release detained U.N. staff.

In the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's Revolutionary Guard repeated its claim that two oil tankers that did not follow its rules struck mines and caught fire. CBS News reports independent agencies have not confirmed the claim. Shipping data firm Kpler says weekly transits through the strait fell from 217 to 147 between November 1st and November 23rd. Jordan's Foreign Ministry says the body of a Jordanian sailor, missing after an attack on a vessel operated by the Abu Dhabi National Oil Company, was found off the coast of the United Arab Emirates. Iran says it will not restore normal shipping until the United States lifts its naval blockade of Iranian ports, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called Iran's government, quote, a sinking ship.

In Ukraine, the Air Force says Russia launched 155 attack drones overnight, and that 110 were shot down or jammed as of 8 this morning, local time. Strikes were recorded at 27 locations. In the city of Izium in the Kharkiv region, local officials say a combined rocket and drone attack killed one person and injured six. President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine has information that Russia is planning another mass strike in the coming days. President Trump said he asked Russian President Vladimir Putin last week to, quote, pull back, and that he hopes for a quick settlement.

And in Brussels, European Union leaders held a summit Thursday, negotiating the bloc's 1.6 trillion euro budget for 2028 through 2034, along with funding for Ukraine.

Now to news here at home.

Thanksgiving storms disrupted travel across much of the country. The Associated Press reports high winds and flooding left more than 800,000 homes and businesses without electricity on Thursday. New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill and New York Governor Kathy Hochul both declared states of emergency, and the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, postponed for the first time because of weather, is now set for Saturday at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. In the Midwest, the first major winter storm of the season brought heavy snow to Missouri and Illinois, with winter storm warnings around St. Louis. On the West Coast, Fox Weather reports record coastal flooding in California, where La Jolla recorded a high tide of more than 8 feet, breaking a record set in October. And in South Florida, Miami recorded more than four and a half inches of rain Wednesday, and a FedEx cargo plane overshot a runway at Miami International Airport. Fox Weather reports no one was hurt, and it was not clear whether weather played a role.

On the economy, the Commerce Department's Bureau of Economic Analysis said Wednesday that the U.S. economy grew at an annual rate of 4.3 percent from July through September, revised up from its first estimate of 3.4 percent. President Trump, in a Thanksgiving Day post on Truth Social, called the difference, quote, a record setting error, and called for an investigation. The Hill notes the revision process is routine, and the bureau's next estimate is due next month. In the same posts, the President wrote that he wanted to thank, quote, the 2 percent of our Somalian population that have a job, and said, quote, the others should be thrown out, immediately. He also accused Democrats of seeking to impose Sharia law in the courts. The Hill did not report responses from Democrats or from the bureau.

Now, Kentucky news.

Thanksgiving was a record wet day. WDRB meteorologist Marc Weinberg reports Louisville officially broke its record for the rainiest November 26th. Some rain mixed with snow in southern Kentucky overnight, but forecasters said no real impacts were expected.

With the cold season arriving, Governor Andy Beshear and state transportation officials say Kentucky is ready for snow and ice. The Transportation Cabinet has nearly 1,500 plow trucks and other snow equipment, about 270,000 tons of salt stored in every county, and more than 1.1 million gallons of brine to cover nearly 28,000 miles of state highways. Beshear said, quote, Team Kentucky's response to winter weather begins with thorough preparation. Transportation Secretary Rebecca Goodman said, quote, rest assured, when the temperature drops and the clouds roll in, our response is ready. Drivers can check road conditions at GoKY dot ky dot gov.

And WKYT reports a Jefferson County family court judge has ruled that former Governor Matt Bevin does not owe child support to his estranged adopted son, who brought the case.

Now, Louisville.

Two physicians, Doctors Gregory and Andrea Woolfolk, have sued the University of Louisville over a medical school scholarship that WDRB reports was affected by Kentucky's law restricting diversity, equity and inclusion programs at public universities. The suit was filed Tuesday in Jefferson Circuit Court.

The owner of a new downtown restaurant says he does not want a shooting at its soft opening to overshadow its purpose. Police say a woman got into a fight with another group of women at Charla's, a brunch restaurant on 7th Street, on Sunday, then pulled a gun and fired, wounding one woman, who was taken to surgery. A woman who police say was invited by a staff member was arrested. Owner Tyler Young named the restaurant for his mother, Charla Young, a former WAVE reporter and talk show host who died in May. He said, quote, I honestly, you know, I hate that it happened. I'm praying for both sides. He plans to fully open in January.

The pilot who suffered life-threatening injuries in Tuesday's plane crash near Bowman Field is expected to make a full recovery, according to his brother, as reported by WDRB.

And the rain did not keep people away from Churchill Downs. WDRB reports the track's Thanksgiving buffet, a tradition since 1969 that draws more than 10,000 people, had a big crowd again on Thursday.

Turning to AI news.

Reuters reports U.S. chip export limits are slowing work at Chinese artificial intelligence companies. According to Reuters, the developer Z dot AI is behind schedule on its next flagship model, and Moonshot AI expects the gap between its major releases to grow from about six months to a year or more if it loses access to computing power overseas. Separately, the tech site The Decoder reports Nvidia told server partners to suspend shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia after tighter U.S. export controls. Nvidia's credit rating is also under review. TechCrunch reports S and P Global is considering lowering Nvidia's rating one step, from double A to double A minus, citing what it called weak executive oversight and a lack of succession planning after Jensen Huang's removal and reinstatement as chief executive. Nvidia would remain investment grade either way.

Forty-two state attorneys general, from both parties, are asking a bankruptcy court to stop Character dot AI from selling users' chat histories. The Verge reports the company's bankruptcy plan would auction chat data from more than 21 million U.S. accounts, including more than 2.1 million registered to minors. The attorneys general say the plan would not require a buyer to honor existing privacy policies or let users delete their data.

New York Governor Kathy Hochul has signed a law banning the sale of toys with built-in AI chatbots for five years, according to NBC News. The law takes effect 90 days after signing, with penalties of 15,000 dollars a day. New York is the second state to do this, after California. Amazon told TechCrunch it will restrict sales where they are prohibited.

For ChatGPT subscribers, OpenAI says it corrected a billing error that had given some users discounts they were not meant to get. The Decoder reports users of its GPT 6.2 models now reach their usage limits faster than they did last week, and OpenAI is giving eligible subscribers an extra usage reset.

And on security, Switzerland's federal cybersecurity office says AI agents, meaning AI programs that can carry out tasks on their own, were used in five recent cyberattacks against Swiss businesses and critical infrastructure, according to Reuters.

Now, the Bengals.

The Bengals held their first full practice of the week on Thanksgiving morning, after a walkthrough Wednesday. Guard Dalton Risner, with a knee injury, did not practice again. Zac Taylor has called him day to day, and A to Z Sports notes it would be unusual for him to play Sunday without practicing at least twice this week. Risner also missed Monday's game in Washington. Defensive tackle Jonathan Allen sat out again for rest. Fellow defensive tackle B.J. Hill was limited, also for rest. Safety Bryan Cook, with an ankle injury, was a full participant for the second straight day, and Taylor expects him to be sore but available day to day. Cornerback Jalen Davis and receiver Andrei Iosivas were full participants again as they work back from injured reserve. A to Z Sports reports both are 14 days into their 21-day practice windows, so the team must decide on activating them by next Thursday. There are no game-status designations yet. Those come with Friday's report.

Defensive coordinator Al Golden pointed to two things from the 22 to 20 loss to Washington, saying, quote, situations and turnovers. He noted the defense held Washington to five field goals and won 8 of 10 third downs, but said, quote, we need to get a fourth down stop or get some takeaways to help our team. Golden on cornerback DJ Turner the Second, who gave up the late catch that set up the winning kick: quote, he'll bounce back. He'll bounce back. Look, it's the NFL, man.

Offensive coordinator Dan Pitcher previewed a Saints pass rush led by Carl Granderson, who has eight and a half sacks, and Chase Young, who has eight. Pitcher said, quote, I think Chase Young is playing at a very high level. Taylor on rookie receiver Colbie Young, who scored his first NFL touchdown Monday: quote, he's just trying to make plays for us, trying to do his job, trying to be available where he can. Taylor also said of first-round pick Shemar Stewart, who was active but did not play Monday, that the way the game unfolded, quote, wasn't any sort of indictment on him. Bengals.com notes the team has not turned the ball over in its last two games.

On the Saints' Thursday report, tight end Juwan Johnson did not practice for a second day, with hamstring and wrist injuries. Center Erik McCoy was upgraded to full participation. Guard David Edwards, tackles Taliese Fuaga and Asim Richards, and linebacker Pete Werner were limited again, and cornerback Martin Emerson Junior was a full participant after a concussion. New Orleans is 3 and 7.

Now, the Bills.

Buffalo beat Kansas City 13 to 10 on Thanksgiving night in the rain, in the first Thanksgiving game ever played in Orchard Park. Tyler Bass kicked a 57-yard field goal as time expired. The winning chance came after Joe Andreessen tipped a Kansas City punt and Greg Dortch returned it to the Chiefs' 39-yard line with 4 seconds left. Bass had also tied the game with a 54-yarder, and Harrison Butker missed a 46-yard try for Kansas City with just under six minutes left. James Cook ran for 117 yards and the Bills' only touchdown. It was a hard night for Josh Allen, who completed 10 of 21 passes for 81 yards with two interceptions. Buffalo's defense held Kansas City to 241 total yards, and Patrick Mahomes threw for 174. The Associated Press game story did not report any Bills injuries. Buffalo is 10 and 1, has won seven straight, and is off until December 6th, at New England.
