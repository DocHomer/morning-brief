Turning to world news.

The White House released a joint declaration on Thursday after the first three-way meeting of President Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin, held in Shenzhen, China. It's called the U.S.-Russia-China Heads of State Declaration toward Peace. It has four points: reforming the United Nations, steady energy supplies, reducing nuclear risk, and opposing what it calls jurisdiction abuse by the International Criminal Court. The three leaders affirmed that, quote, a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought, and agreed to a three-way dialogue on nuclear risk. ABC News reports the declaration includes no cuts to nuclear arsenals, no verification and no timetable, and it does not mention Ukraine or the war with Iran. On nuclear weapons, President Trump said, quote, it's not an easy thing to do, give them up. Putin said the countries have numerous shared interests in settling regional conflicts. China's readout called the meeting a groundbreaking move. The president left China without taking questions and says he'll hold a news conference at the White House today.

In Berlin, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz hosted nearly 40 delegations backing Ukraine, along with French President Emmanuel Macron, British Prime Minister Andy Burnham and U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. Kushner said Russia has agreed to talks that include European representatives. The Kyiv Post notes Russia has not confirmed that. Kushner said a security guarantee similar to NATO's Article Five would be, quote, a really, really big deal, and that the president is open to it if it serves American interests. Merz said, quote, we want to be involved in these negotiations. Macron said the question of Ukraine joining NATO should be reopened. The European Commission proposed a 45 billion euro loan to Ukraine for next year. On the same day, Russian strikes on Kyiv killed one person, injured at least four, and damaged St. Volodymyr's Cathedral and the Austrian and Dutch embassies. Ukraine struck the bridge to Crimea for the third time in a week, and rail service across it was suspended. Zelenskyy is set to meet Witkoff and Kushner about the next round of talks. No date has been set.

In the Middle East, President Trump told Israel's Channel 12 on Thursday, quote, I'm not rushing to make any decisions on Iran, and that he'll see what happens after the new year starts. Bloomberg and The New York Times had reported new U.S. strikes were becoming more likely. The Pentagon says the war has cost more than 48 billion dollars since late February. CBS News reports a strike by Yemen's Houthi rebels damaged the Riyadh tower that houses the headquarters of Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund. No one was hurt, and Saudi Arabia condemned the attack. Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed two more tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz, which CBS notes is a claim rarely confirmed. And the head of the International Energy Agency, Fatih Birol, called the Hormuz crisis, quote, the largest threat to energy security in history.

The United Nations' top court ruled Thursday that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, a mostly Muslim minority. The International Court of Justice ruled 9 to 6 in a case brought by The Gambia. The court found Myanmar's forces killed thousands, committed widespread sexual violence and violence against children, and systematically destroyed villages. Court President Yuji Iwasawa said, quote, Myanmar thus violated its obligation to prevent genocide. U.N. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres called it a powerful recognition of the victims. Myanmar has denied the charges and called its actions clearance operations against armed attacks. The court ordered Myanmar to prosecute those responsible and submit a reparations plan by November 2028. The court cannot enforce its rulings, but The Gambia could take the matter to the U.N. Security Council.

The Senate passed its permitting overhaul Thursday night, 62 to 33. The bill speeds environmental reviews, accelerates new power lines, and limits agencies' power to revoke existing permits. New data centers of 20 megawatts or more would have to pay the full cost of the power infrastructure built to serve them. Co-author Shelley Moore Capito, a West Virginia Republican, pointed to what she called endless red tape. Co-author Sheldon Whitehouse, a Rhode Island Democrat, said, quote, Americans will start to get some relief quickly if this bill passes. Most of the no votes came from Democrats, and some environmental and tribal groups oppose it. President Trump supports it. It now goes to the House, where Majority Leader Steve Scalise said, quote, the details matter.

A federal judge in Tampa ruled Thursday that President Trump's 15 billion dollar defamation lawsuit against The New York Times can go forward. Judge Steven Merryday dismissed two claims and narrowed others. What remains involves statements accusing the president of tax fraud, tax evasion or money laundering. The judge found the president had adequately alleged actual malice, the legal standard for public figures. The ruling is not a finding that the Times defamed him. The Times has argued the suit is aimed at, quote, scoring political points. The Times could appeal, or the case could head toward trial.

And in South Florida, a stalled storm dumped more than 15 inches of rain in three days on parts of Broward County. Broward, Fort Lauderdale, Hollywood and Tamarac declared emergencies. Governor Ron DeSantis sent hundreds of Guard members, more than 200 state troopers and dozens of pumps and high-water vehicles. Wildlife officers rescued more than 70 people and 20 to 30 pets from one apartment complex in Hollywood. No deaths have been reported. Broward schools are closed until November 30. Drier weather arrives today, but seasonal high tides next week could bring more flooding.

Turning to Kentucky.

Governor Andy Beshear said Thursday he's concerned about a federal ban on intoxicating hemp products that takes effect December 11. The federal limit bars products with more than four-tenths of a milligram of total T H C per container. WLKY reports most T H C drinks on shelves now have 5 to 10 milligrams. Beshear said Kentucky should be allowed to regulate hemp itself and warned the ban could bankrupt the industry. Senator Mitch McConnell, who championed hemp legalization in 2018, has called the high-T H C products an unintended consequence. He closed that loophole in a spending bill last year. Some lawmakers are lobbying Congress, but the December 11 date stands.

Kentucky is switching back to the A C T. Starting next spring, public school juniors will take the A C T as the state-funded college entrance exam, after one year of the S A T. The Kentucky Department of Education signed a four-year contract at 28 dollars and 50 cents per student and estimates it will save nearly 500,000 dollars a year compared with 2025. Education Commissioner Robbie Fletcher said the department is thankful the process is complete, so schools know which test juniors will take.

Kentucky Power has asked state regulators to approve a 2.8 billion dollar natural gas plant to serve a proposed data center in Boyd and Greenup counties. The Kentucky Lantern reports the data center company, TeraWulf, would pay about 100 million dollars over 10 years to offset residential winter bills, which the utility estimates at about 26 dollars a month per customer in winter. Kentucky Power President Cindy Wiseman said the company wants customers to share directly in the benefits. Boyd County ratepayer Suzanne Griffith said residents are, quote, at their utter limits. The Public Service Commission will decide, and the plant would open in 2031.
