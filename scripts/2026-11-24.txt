Turning to world news.

Ukraine says it sank a Russian warship more than 1,500 miles from the front line. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Monday that seven Ukrainian-made naval drones struck the Russian corvette Merkury at the Benghazi naval base in eastern Libya. He said a Russian military helicopter was also destroyed, and that Ukrainian forces hit a sanctioned Russian cargo ship, the Angara, off Libya on Sunday. Zelenskyy said Moscow used that ship to move weapons, and he said, quote, despite all the enemy's efforts to counter the attack, the Russian corvette Merkury was sunk. Libya's eastern-based government said a drone likely caused the explosion. The United Nations mission in Libya called for an independent investigation and said Libya, quote, must not become a battleground for foreign conflicts. The Kremlin made no immediate comment on the Libya strikes. No casualties were reported.

NATO has granted part of Poland's request for help after three Russian cruise missiles came down on Polish territory on Friday. After a meeting of NATO ambassadors in Brussels on Monday, the alliance agreed to place an AWACS early warning surveillance aircraft at Poland's disposal. Other Polish requests, including more NATO fighter jets, were pushed to a meeting of NATO foreign ministers next week. Polish Deputy Defense Minister Pawel Zalewski said, quote, from all sides, I heard expressions of support. Prime Minister Donald Tusk called the incident a premeditated provocation. NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte called it a dangerous incident but said he would never speculate on whether it was intentional.

In the Persian Gulf, Iranian state media reported that parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said today Iran will not negotiate with the United States until seven conditions are met, including a permanent end to hostilities, the lifting of sanctions, and removal of the U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports. Iran's Revolutionary Guard navy said three oil tankers were damaged after hitting naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz. That claim has not been independently confirmed. On Monday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced sanctions on 22 companies and 8 people that Treasury says form Iran's shadow banking network, calling it, quote, the last financial lifeline of a bankrupt regime. President Trump has said Iran wants a deal but has not offered acceptable terms.

The head of the World Food Programme warned the U.N. Security Council on Monday that wars are blocking three major shipping routes for food: the Strait of Hormuz, the Bab al-Mandeb strait near Yemen, and the Black Sea. Executive Director Luke Lindberg said the agency expects 727 million dollars in extra costs by mid-2027, which could put aid for 7.3 million people at risk. He said, quote, grain is available, but it is not reaching the people who need it.

In Belgium, police say 50,000 people marched in Brussels on Monday during a national strike against the government's planned budget cuts. The strike grounded all flights in and out of the country and disrupted transit and schools. Police used tear gas and water cannon, and at least one person was arrested. And in Bangladesh, former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina plans to return from exile in India on December 16th, according to her spokesperson. Now to news here in the U.S.

President Trump on Monday signed a directive ordering the Justice Department to seek the death penalty in murder cases involving children, rape and human trafficking, and telling Attorney General Todd Blanche to expedite executions of federal death row inmates. Trump also urged Congress to pass the Just Punishment for Murder Act. He criticized California Governor Gavin Newsom, who commuted the death sentences of 548 people earlier this month, saying Newsom, quote, stole away the only closure victims' families had. Newsom has said the state cannot morally execute those prisoners, and he released statements from victims' relatives who support his decision. In Iowa, a federal appeals court on Monday cleared the Army to detonate nearly five tons of deteriorating lead-based explosives at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa. The ruling came about five hours after President Trump posted that he would not allow the blast until courts approved and Iowa's governor-elect agreed. Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle said the Army will proceed, saying, quote, every expert agency agrees. Governor-elect Rob Sand said, quote, no good options exist. Local environmental groups argue residents got only days of notice. The American Lung Association advised residents to stay indoors for 24 to 48 hours after the blast. The exact date has not been confirmed.

Now, Louisville.

Louisville Metro has launched a 450,000-dollar pilot program to keep families with young children from losing their homes. The Home for Good prevention pilot offers up to 1,500 dollars for rent, a security deposit or utilities. It's for Jefferson County households with a child under 5, or a child under 18 with a disability, earning up to about 66,000 dollars a year for a family of four. The Louisville Neighborhood Network runs it. Mayor Craig Greenberg said recipients will also be connected with a social worker or a support organization. The network's executive director, Clare Rutz Wallace, said that today families, quote, have to become homeless before we can help them avoid homelessness.

Now, the latest in artificial intelligence.

Nvidia, the company whose chips power most of today's A.I. systems, has reinstated co-founder Jensen Huang as chief executive, 11 days after he resigned. Nvidia's board had said Huang disclosed personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, and news reports had linked him to a woman U.S. officials say was recruited by Chinese intelligence. China denies that. In a filing Monday, the board said it was satisfied with Huang's candor and, after input from senior U.S. government officials, concluded his return poses no national security risk. Nvidia is creating a new board committee to oversee export controls, which are the U.S. rules on which advanced chips can be sold to countries like China, and it is hiring a chief compliance officer. Huang said, quote, I take this responsibility with the greatest humility. Nvidia's stock closed up about 31 percent, and the major stock indexes had their best day since April of last year. Huang is scheduled to answer lawmakers' questions privately on December 1st and 2nd about chip smuggling and export controls.

Virginia and Oregon have become the first states to sue over sexual deepfake images made with Grok, the chatbot from xAI. The lawsuits, filed Monday, target SpaceX, the parent company of both xAI and the social platform X. The states allege a Grok feature let users turn photos of real people, including children, into sexual images, and that the company misled the public about its safeguards. Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones said the weak safeguards were, quote, not a failure but a feature. Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield said the company knowingly designed a product without necessary safeguards. SpaceX did not immediately respond. X's safety team said it has blocked such requests and works with law enforcement.

And two A.I. companies have each posted what they say is a proof of a 47-year-old math problem, known as the V.P. versus V.N.P. conjecture, a cousin of the famous P versus N.P. question about whether some problems are fundamentally harder to compute than others. According to trade publications, Anthropic posted a 70-page paper credited to its Claude model, along with a version written for Lean, software that checks each step of a proof mechanically. A few hours later, OpenAI posted its own proof, which it says it reached on November 6th. Anthropic's own posting notes that no human mathematician has yet checked the proof, and experts still need to confirm that the computer-checked statement matches the original problem.

Now, the Bengals.

A five-game winning streak is over. The Commanders beat the Bengals 22 to 20 on Monday Night Football in Landover, Maryland, on a Drew Stevens field goal as time ran out. It was Stevens' fifth field goal of the night, all in the second half. The Bengals fall to 7 and 3, and Washington is 4, 5 and 1.

Cincinnati controlled the first half. Evan McPherson hit a 38-yard field goal, and Joe Burrow found Tee Higgins for a 31-yard touchdown to make it 10 to nothing. Washington scored a touchdown with 12 seconds left before halftime. Burrow then opened the second half with a 4-yard touchdown pass to rookie Colbie Young, the first of Young's career. But Washington scored on six straight drives after halftime. The Commanders pulled within 17 to 16 with about nine minutes left, then took a 19 to 17 lead on a 41-yard field goal on fourth and 1 with just over two minutes to go. Burrow answered, hitting Mike Gesicki for 32 yards to set up a 56-yard McPherson field goal with 46 seconds left, putting the Bengals back in front 20 to 19. Then Jayden Daniels hit Antonio Williams for gains of 23 and 42 yards, and Stevens kicked the winner from 26 yards out, according to the Associated Press.

There was some confusion at the very end. The clock should have stopped with about two seconds left when the kick hit the net, but the stadium clock ran out and the game ended. E.S.P.N. rules analyst Russell Yurk said that once it reached zero, replay could not fix it, and that the game properly ended. Several outlets noted the Bengals would only have had a kickoff return left. No statement from the Bengals or the league on the clock was found.

Burrow went 28 of 40 for 300 yards, two touchdowns and no interceptions. Fox 19's Charlie Goldsmith reports Washington blitzed on more than 80 percent of Burrow's dropbacks and often double-teamed Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins. Chase had 5 catches for 31 yards on 9 targets. Gesicki led the team with 77 receiving yards, and Samaje Perine had a career-long 44-yard catch and run. The running game managed just 31 yards on 14 carries. On defense, Boye Mafe had three sacks, all in the first half, a career high. He now has nine and a half sacks this season. Daniels threw for 289 yards and ran for 55, and Antonio Williams caught 9 passes for 122 yards.

The late decisions drew questions. Leading 17 to 16 with about 10 minutes left, the Bengals threw five straight passes and punted, as the Enquirer's Jason Williams noted. And trailing 19 to 17, they threw on second and third down before the field goal that left Washington 46 seconds. Zac Taylor explained the plan, saying, quote, we wanted to throw the ball, get the first down, and kick a field goal to win. He added that on the run game, there was, quote, maybe a little bit more of an opportunity than what we took advantage of. Burrow took responsibility for the third-down sack, saying, quote, if we get the first down, we don't give them a chance to have the ball. I made a really bad play. Chase said, quote, one loss don't determine nothing. And Gesicki said, quote, everybody's used to the Bengals scoring 30-plus. The loss ended a run of five straight games with at least 31 points.

On injuries, safety Bryan Cook hurt his ankle in the first half, was listed questionable to return, and came back for the second half, according to Bengals dot com's Geoff Hobson. It's his third ankle issue this season. Cornerback Dax Hill, listed with an illness, sat out the final two defensive drives, according to Goldsmith. Taylor gave no new injury updates after the game. Before the game, the team activated linebacker Swayze Bozeman and guard Brian Parker the Second from injured reserve.
