Turning to world news.

The U.S. military says it disabled another ship trying to get past its naval blockade of Iranian ports. U.S. Central Command said a military aircraft fired precision munitions at the stern of the Palau-flagged cargo ship Pacific Archer on Thursday, knocking out its propulsion. Central Command said the ship had left the Iranian port of Bandar Abbas, and that the civilian crew was not hurt. The Associated Press reports it is at least the 15th ship the United States has disabled since the blockade began in April. Iran's navy commander, Rear Admiral Shahram Irani, said on state television that Iran will carry out operations to break the blockade. He also claimed Iranian commandos retook two oil tankers seized by U.S. forces, but gave no evidence, and CBS News notes the United States has not announced seizing any Iran-linked commercial ship since April. The energy research firm Rystad Energy says oil flows in the first two weeks of November fell about 20 percent from October. President Trump said Thursday at Mar-a-Lago, quote, we have victory at hand, and said he is giving Iran a chance at a deal but doubts it will take it.

In Ukraine, the Air Force says Russia launched 162 drones overnight, and that 141 were shot down by 8 this morning, local time. The Kyiv Independent reports Russian attacks killed at least 10 people and injured 55 across Ukraine over the past day. In the city of Nizhyn, in the Chernihiv region, a strike on local businesses killed seven people. Rescuers pulled one person alive from the rubble. Separately, President Volodymyr Zelensky said nine Ukrainian children were returned from Russian-controlled territory through the mediation of First Lady Melania Trump. The youngest is 8. Zelensky thanked her and said Ukraine has now secured the return of 2,875 children. And Germany approved nearly 12 billion euros in support for Ukraine for 2027.

In Gaza, the medical charity Doctors Without Borders says an Israeli strike on one of its clinics in Gaza City killed two of its watchmen. The group said the site was clearly marked with its logos, that the Israeli military knew its location, and that 17 of its staff have now been killed in Gaza. The Israeli military said, without providing evidence, that the two men were Hamas members. World Health Organization chief Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus called the strike, quote, outrageous and unacceptable. A ceasefire has been in effect since October of last year.

Canada's Supreme Court has largely upheld Quebec's law banning religious symbols at work for public school teachers, police officers and some other public employees. The court made one exception, ruling that members of Quebec's legislature cannot be required to uncover their faces. Chief Justice Richard Wagner wrote that the law reflects Quebec's, quote, legitimate and historical commitment to a secular state. Two justices partly dissented, writing that the law violates freedom of religion and that its effects, quote, fall most heavily on Muslim women.

In Venezuela, National Assembly speaker Jorge Rodríguez says lawmakers will repeal a law that permanently barred from office anyone who supported foreign sanctions. He expects a vote next week. Critics say the law was used against opposition figures. Rodríguez said no politician has been subjected to it. The country's interim government, led by his sister, Delcy Rodríguez, has been negotiating with opposition parties since August, and has promised elections but has not set a date.

Now to news here at home.

Black Friday set an online spending record. Adobe Digital Insights says Americans spent 9.7 billion dollars online by 6:30 Friday evening, up nearly 13 percent from last year, and expects the day's final total to reach 13 billion dollars or more. Thanksgiving Day online spending was also a record, 7.1 billion dollars. In stores, the Associated Press reports crowds were mixed. Mall of America said about 15,000 shoppers came through in the first hour, up 8 and a half percent, while some other stores reported lighter crowds. The National Retail Federation expects holiday sales of just over 1 trillion dollars, up about 4 percent, and says higher prices account for about half of that growth. One shopper, a retired veteran from Illinois, told the AP food prices are, quote, astronomical.

The weather cleanup continues. USA Today's tracker showed more than 540,000 homes and businesses still without power Friday afternoon, mostly in Maryland, Missouri, New Jersey, New York, Pennsylvania and Virginia. Parts of Missouri and Illinois got up to 15 inches of snow. Fox Weather says another storm develops over the Plains tonight into Sunday, with rain, thunderstorms and strong winds spreading across the Mississippi Valley, Midwest and South on one of the busiest travel days of the holiday. And the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, postponed for the first time in its history, steps off in New York this morning at 8:30 Eastern on NBC and Peacock. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani will miss it. He had surgery Friday to repair a torn Achilles tendon, and his office says he is recovering at Gracie Mansion and is expected to fully recover.

Reuters reports hackers breached Cloud One, the Air Force's main cloud computing platform. Three sources told Reuters the intrusion was found within roughly the past month and may have put tactical, personnel and logistics data at risk. Reuters could not determine who was responsible or whether data was taken. The Air Force declined to comment on specific systems and said it routinely takes protective measures.

In Washington, House Republican leaders plan a vote the week of November 30th on a bipartisan bill to speed up permits for energy projects, which the Senate passed last week. Speaker Mike Johnson told members he needs, quote, full attendance. Democratic Senator Martin Heinrich, a co-sponsor, said there is no alternative that can become law without Republican votes. Seven House Democrats who lead tribal and natural resource panels called the bill, quote, fatally flawed, citing weaker environmental rules.

And a spokesperson for Nebraska Republican Senator Deb Fischer says her husband, Bruce Fischer, a Sandhills rancher, died by suicide last weekend. The family said it is deeply grateful for the outpouring of sympathy. If you or someone you know is struggling, you can call or text 988.

Now, Kentucky news.

The Kentucky Lantern reports changes to the federal food assistance program, known as SNAP, are straining food pantries across the state. About 80,000 Kentuckians have lost access to food benefits. Vincent James, president of Dare to Care in Louisville, said more than 200,000 people in its service area now face food insecurity, the first time that number has topped 200,000. James said, quote, for those who are on the margins, they are now over. God's Pantry, which serves 50 counties, says it has served 314,000 people so far this year, up from about 280,000 last year. Melissa McDonald of Feeding Kentucky said SNAP provides nine meals for every one a food bank can provide.

Earlier this week, Governor Andy Beshear created a new state Office of Outdoor Recreation by executive order. It will run the Kentucky Trail Town Program and work to draw more visitors for hiking, biking and other outdoor activities. Beshear called tourism a record 14.6 billion dollar industry. Seth Wheat, who worked for Kentucky State Parks, will be the executive director.

And a Louisville high school receiver set a national record. Christian Academy of Louisville senior Ja'Hyde Brown caught his 40th touchdown pass of the season in a 49 to nothing win over previously unbeaten Rockcastle County in the Class 3A semifinals, breaking the national single-season record of 39. It was also his 70th career touchdown catch, a Kentucky record. Coach Hunter Cantwell said, quote, just witnessing history. CAL plays Lexington Catholic for the state title on December 5th at Kroger Field.

Now, Louisville.

Seven people were shot late Friday night at what police believe was a teenager's birthday party at the Mellwood Art Center in Clifton Heights. Louisville Metro Police say it happened around 10:40 p.m. in the 1800 block of Mellwood Avenue, after an argument. Two teenagers and five adults were shot, and all are expected to survive. No suspect information has been released. Police said, quote, what should have been an evening of celebration unfortunately turned into an evening of violence. They are asking anyone who was there, or who has cellphone video, to call the anonymous tip line at 574-LMPD.

Mayor Craig Greenberg made on-street parking free on Black Friday in NuLu, the Highlands and downtown to bring shoppers to local businesses. Katie Meinhart, president of the NuLu Business Association, told WLKY she would like the city to bring back free two-hour parking, saying, quote, we really need that support to get us through January and February.

In Middletown, police say a 21-year-old woman tried to push a cart with more than 600 dollars in merchandise out of the Target on Shelbyville Road early Friday, then resisted arrest. WDRB reports two officers had minor injuries. She is charged with shoplifting, resisting arrest and assault of a police officer.

And the holiday season is underway. The eighth annual Fête de Noël opened Friday in Paristown, with ice skating, a trackless train, Santa and a holiday market with 20 local businesses. It runs through January 3rd. Light Up Louisville is next Saturday, December 5th, from 3 to 9 p.m. at Jefferson Square Park, with the parade at 6 and the tree lighting around 8.

Turning to AI news.

OpenAI has filed counterclaims against Apple in a California court. The Financial Times first reported, and Reuters relayed, that OpenAI accuses Apple of stealing its trade secrets and of blocking its access to hardware suppliers. 9 to 5 Mac reports OpenAI alleges Apple is, quote, coercing and threatening suppliers that work with OpenAI on its planned personal devices. Apple sued OpenAI in July, alleging former Apple employees took confidential hardware details to help OpenAI's device work. OpenAI denies that. Reuters said neither company immediately responded to requests for comment. Apple's case is set for trial in July 2027.

Bloomberg reports Nvidia's top-end Blackwell chips, which the U.S. does not allow to be exported to China, were installed at a data center site in Shanghai. According to Bloomberg, loan documents filed with Chinese regulators list a Blackwell server rack, which holds 72 of the chips, by serial number, pledged as collateral for a bank loan of about 14 million dollars. Bloomberg says the site is linked to Alice Huang, founder of Megaspeed, a Singapore cloud company the U.S. is investigating over alleged chip smuggling and placed on a trade blacklist this month. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing. Taiwan's Gigabyte built the rack and says it follows all applicable laws. Bloomberg could not determine how it reached China. Nvidia did not respond.

AI tools played a bigger part in holiday shopping this year. Adobe says traffic to U.S. retail websites from AI sources, meaning chatbots and AI assistants, rose 162 percent from last year on Thanksgiving, and that those visitors were 74 percent more likely to buy something. Salesforce estimates AI agents, programs that can search and act on a shopper's behalf, could influence about one-fifth of global online sales during this Cyber Week, but says only 8 percent of consumers have let an AI agent actually make a purchase for them.

In Canada, Artificial Intelligence Minister Evan Solomon announced an additional 700 million Canadian dollars for a fund that helps businesses pay for the computing power used to build and run AI, bringing it to 1 billion. Companies can now recover up to 80 percent of costs if they buy from Canadian-owned cloud providers, and up to 50 percent from other providers based in Canada.
