Good morning, this is The Morning Brief for Wednesday, November 18. Today: the United States strikes an Iranian missile site on Qeshm Island, Nvidia beats expectations in its first report under an interim chief executive, Louisville gets a high-tech fix for the Can Opener bridge, and the U.S. Men's National Team gives Mauricio Pochettino a winning sendoff in Cincinnati.

There are no weather alerts. Tonight is clear, with a low around 36. Thursday is sunny, with a high near 65 and a low around 38. Friday is sunny again and a little warmer, with a high near 67 and a low around 40. Saturday is mostly sunny, with a high near 66 and only a slight chance of rain.

Turning to world news.

The United States carried out what a U.S. military official called precision strikes on a missile site of Iran's Revolutionary Guards on Qeshm Island, in the Strait of Hormuz, on Tuesday afternoon. The official described the strikes as self-defense against Revolutionary Guard aggression targeting U.S. personnel and assets. U.S. Central Command called Iran's claim that it hit an American radar in Bahrain false, and said the drones were intercepted with no damage or injuries. Iran's armed forces said three tankers struck naval mines in the strait on Tuesday. The U.S. says it has cleared the main shipping routes.

In Ukraine, Russian missile strikes overnight Monday into Tuesday hit Kyiv's Northern and Southern bridges over the Dnipro River, closing both to traffic. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia used seven ballistic missiles. That came after Ukraine's strike on the Kerch Bridge to Crimea. Zelenskyy said Ukraine is, quote, reminding Russia that war will always return to where it began. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said the Kerch Bridge will be restored and will be even better. On the diplomatic front, Axios and Reuters report that White House envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner plan to meet Ukrainian and European officials in Berlin on Thursday.

President Trump is in Manila for meetings with Southeast Asian leaders. On Tuesday he held his first meeting this term with the leaders of the so-called Quad: Australia, India and Japan. The president said, quote, our countries are the four corners of a free, open, stable, and prosperous Indo-Pacific. The president and Thailand's prime minister signed a joint statement laying out proposed terms of a trade deal, under which Thailand would drop tariffs on U.S. exports and buy about 2.6 billion dollars more in American farm goods. The president said he expects a formal deal within weeks. Today the president meets Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos, then heads to the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, China.

Now to news from around the country.

Nvidia reported results after the closing bell Tuesday, its first quarterly report since chief executive Jensen Huang's resignation. Revenue was 115.8 billion dollars, about 7 billion dollars above Wall Street's expectations. The company forecast about 130 billion dollars in revenue next quarter. Interim chief executive Colette Kress said, quote, our momentum is real, our demand is accelerating, our supply is growing. Shares rose 3 to 5 percent in after-hours trading.

Before that report, stocks fell for a second straight day. The Dow dropped 849 points, or 1.6 percent, to close at 50,759. The S and P 500 fell 1 percent and the Nasdaq fell seven-tenths of a percent. The Associated Press said artificial intelligence stocks and financial companies led the declines.

In California, the second of two atmospheric river storms was due to reach the coast overnight. Fox Weather reports more than 30 million people face a flash flood threat through Thursday. The San Francisco Bay Area could see 2 to 3 inches of rain today, with coastal wind gusts of 40 to 60 miles an hour.

The Senate's permitting reform bill is still awaiting a final vote, expected before the Thanksgiving recess. Democratic Senator Catherine Cortez Masto of Nevada says she will vote no unless a historic preservation provision is removed. Republican supporters, led by Senator Shelley Moore Capito, say the bill will cut the time needed to build transmission lines and energy projects.

At the Supreme Court, the justices on Monday declined to hear three appeals related to diversity, equity and inclusion, including the Trump administration's appeal over canceled University of California research grants. That leaves a lower court order in place.

Turning to Kentucky.

A new American Lung Association report finds Kentucky has the highest rate of new lung cancer cases in the country, at about 84 per 100,000 people, compared with 52 nationally. That rate is down 7 percent over five years. Kentucky ranks 38th of 43 states for five-year survival.

The Trump administration is giving states more time to put new Medicaid work requirements in place, but the Kentucky Lantern reports Kentucky can't take the delay. A spokesperson for the state Cabinet for Health and Family Services said state law, passed as House Bill 2, requires Kentucky to begin on January 1. The rule requires many adults ages 19 to 64 to log 80 hours a month of work, community service or school, with some exemptions. The state estimates 250,000 Kentuckians could lose coverage. Medicaid covers about 1.4 million people in Kentucky.

In western Kentucky, the Lantern reports the Pennyrile Regional Energy Agency has spent more than 1.7 million dollars of state money over the past year on a proposed 53-mile natural gas pipeline through Todd, Christian, Trigg, Caldwell and Lyon counties, including legal fees for eminent domain lawsuits against landowners. Lyon County farmer Milton Cook, who is being sued for his land, said his tax dollars are being used to condemn his property on a project that will have no benefit to him.

And a federal magistrate judge found that former Rowan County Clerk Kim Davis violated court rules by failing to disclose assets and property transfers in the long-running lawsuit by a couple she refused a marriage license in 2015. She owes them about 565,000 dollars in damages, fees and interest. The judge declined to hold her in contempt but ordered updated answers. The judge will next decide on sanctions.

Now, Louisville news.

Mayor Craig Greenberg announced a 3 million dollar plan to warn truck drivers before they hit the railroad bridge at Third Street and Eastern Parkway known as the Can Opener. The U.S. Department of Transportation is providing a 2 million dollar grant, and Metro government is adding 1 million. Cameras will detect vehicles too tall for the 11-foot, 8-inch clearance and warn drivers with new signs. At least 45 trucks hit or got stuck under the bridge during this summer's I-65 closure. Greenberg said the goal is to make the Can Opener, quote, part of Louisville's history, not our future. The work is expected to be done next spring.

A woman was killed and a man critically hurt in a shooting in Smoketown Tuesday night. It happened around 7:30 in the 700 block of East Broadway. Police say early information points to gunfire exchanged between people in a vehicle and people in a nearby parking lot. The man was taken to UofL Hospital. Neither victim has been named. LMPD's homicide unit is investigating.

Police have charged a 46-year-old man, Marcus Greene, with complicity to murder in a February 2025 shooting outside a Wendy's on West Broadway in the Russell neighborhood.

The Louisville Zoo says several bird areas remain closed after two white storks were lost to bird flu, including Lorikeet Landing, the Forest Bird Trail and the African penguin exhibits. The zoo itself remains open daily from 10 to 4.

Now, the Bengals.

The Bengals are 7 and 2 after Sunday night's 38 to 18 win over Pittsburgh, and getting ready for Monday Night Football in Washington.

The team's official numbers have Joe Burrow at 35 of 44 for 408 yards and four touchdowns. Burrow is completing 74.7 percent of his passes. Geoff Hobson of Bengals.com notes that would be the best full-season mark in NFL history, ahead of Drew Brees at 74.4 percent. Burrow has 27 touchdown passes and is on pace for 50. Pittsburgh came in with 16 sacks during its four-game winning streak, and got just one on Sunday.

Burrow is up for the FedEx Air Player of the Week award again after winning it the week before. Fan voting closes today at 3 p.m. Eastern.

Offensive coordinator Dan Pitcher had a message for opposing defenses. Quote: Every time they blitz us, it is a big play opportunity for us. He added, please blitz us, because we have just supreme confidence in our guys' ability to protect it. That matters this week. Hobson notes Washington blitzes on 35.7 percent of dropbacks, fourth-most in the league, according to Pro Football Reference.

Heltman also reports guard Dylan Fairchild is being monitored but is not ruled out, Tee Higgins is fine after some pain during the game, and safety Kyle Dugger, who has a quad injury, has played in only four games this season. Heltman writes that receiver Andrei Iosivas, cornerback Jalen Davis and guard Brian Parker the Second, who are in their practice windows off injured reserve, may return this week. As for the opponent, Washington beat the Giants 24 to 21 last Thursday for its third win of the season. Jayden Daniels is expected to play. The betting line has moved Cincinnati's way. Peter Dewey of SI Betting reports DraftKings has the Bengals favored by 6 and a half points, with a total of 52 and a half.

In the standings, the Bengals lead the AFC North at 7 and 2, a game ahead of Pittsburgh at 6 and 3. Cleveland is 6 and 4 and Baltimore 4 and 6. In the AFC, Buffalo is the top seed at 8 and 1. Now, the U.S. Men's National Team.

The U.S. closed out its November window Tuesday night in Cincinnati. USA 2, Haiti 0, at TQL Stadium in front of 24,001 fans. That completed an 8 to nothing aggregate win in the Concacaf Nations League quarterfinals, after a 6 to nothing win in the first leg on Saturday.

Christian Pulisic opened the scoring in the 25th minute with a bicycle kick off a lofted cross from Weston McKennie. Pulisic scored three goals across the two legs and has 36 for his country. Then 18-year-old Julian Hall finished another McKennie assist in the 43rd minute. Hall, who started ahead of Folarin Balogun, has scored in all four of his starts and has five goals in six caps.

The win sends the U.S. to the Nations League Finals in Los Angeles in March, and clinches a spot in the 2027 Gold Cup.

It was also Mauricio Pochettino's final match. He leaves to coach Manchester United, starting today. Pochettino finishes with a record of 23 wins, 12 losses and 2 draws, including a round of 16 exit at this summer's World Cup after a 4 to 1 loss to Belgium. After the match he said, quote, I am sure that this group of players, they will achieve big things in the future. I have no doubt.

U.S. Soccer chief executive JT Batson says the federation is searching for a coach who values youth development. No permanent successor has been named. ESPN reports former U.S. international Steve Cherundolo will oversee the next match. That's a friendly against Jamaica on Sunday, December 13, in Fort Lauderdale, Florida. Kickoff time and TV details are still to come.

And finally, some good news.

In south Louisville, a mother of three who spent four months without a home is now in a fully furnished house. Alaina Norwood and her children had been staying at the Volunteers of America Community Care Campus. Happy Homes Furniture Bank and Furniture Fair furnished the home. Norwood's 8-year-old son, Asher, asked for one thing: a kitchen table, because the family had been eating on the floor. He got it. Norwood told WLKY, I couldn't believe that it was something that was true.

And researchers at M I T found that quiet bursts of sound, timed to the brain's slow waves during deep sleep, made those waves larger and strengthened the flow of fluid that helps clear waste from the brain. The small study of 14 healthy adults was published in Science Translational Medicine. The scientists say they have not yet shown more waste is removed, and the lead author has started a company working toward a headband people could use at home.
