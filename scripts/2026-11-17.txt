Good morning, this is The Morning Brief for Tuesday, November 17. Today: the Bengals beat the Steelers 38 to 18 on Sunday night to move to 7 and 2, Ukraine strikes the Kerch Bridge to Crimea, the Senate advances a major permitting bill, and Congressman James Comer launches a run for Kentucky governor.

Let's start with the sports roundup.

On Monday Night Football, the Los Angeles Chargers got their first win of the season. Chargers 23, Ravens 20. Justin Herbert threw for 129 yards and ran for 51 more and a touchdown, and Omarion Hampton ran 22 times for 105 yards and a score. The deciding play came with 2 minutes and 3 seconds left, when Tarheeb Still stripped the ball from Zay Flowers. Jacoby Brissett, making his Ravens debut after a trade from Arizona, threw for 340 yards. The Chargers are 1 and 8. Baltimore has lost five straight and is 4 and 6.

On Sunday, the 49ers beat the Cowboys 46 to 40, with Brock Purdy throwing for 389 yards. The Chiefs beat the Falcons 35 to 13 behind five touchdown passes from Patrick Mahomes. The Lions beat the Patriots 35 to 10 in Munich, though Detroit's Aidan Hutchinson hurt his knee and New England's Drake Maye hurt his ankle. Cleveland came back from 17 to nothing down to beat Houston 27 to 24, and Jacksonville beat Tennessee 40 to 23 to reach 7 and 2 for the first time since 1999.

In the NBA, Golden State's Stephen Curry will miss at least two weeks with a torn right oblique muscle. Also, Trae Young was held scoreless Sunday for the first time in his career, ending a streak of 511 straight games with at least one point.

In college football, the new Associated Press Top 25 has Georgia at number one, followed by Utah, Notre Dame, UCLA and Miami. LSU and South Carolina dropped out. The College Football Playoff committee releases its next rankings tonight on ESPN, around 9 p.m. Eastern.

Turning to world news.

Ukraine struck the Kerch Bridge, which links Russia to occupied Crimea. Attacks began Monday and continued overnight into Tuesday, according to NBC News. Geolocated video shows one section of the bridge collapsed into the water. Ukraine's SBU security service said a metal arch and the road span beneath it were destroyed and the rail section was damaged. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called it an unprecedented operation. Russia halted traffic on the bridge and opened a criminal case for what it called a terrorist act. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said the bridge will be rebuilt. In Kyiv, Russian overnight strikes damaged and closed two main bridges and killed two people, and strikes on the power grid were reported in eight regions. U.S.-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi last week ended without a breakthrough.

In the Persian Gulf, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they attacked a U.S. radar at Sheikh Isa air base in Bahrain with drones. Bahrain said its air defenses intercepted the attack with no casualties or damage. Secretary of State Marco Rubio condemned the strikes in a call with Bahrain's foreign minister. Iran's foreign minister met Oman's foreign minister in Muscat on Tuesday to discuss protecting shipping lanes.

President Trump arrived in Manila on Tuesday to begin a three-day Asia trip for meetings of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations. On Thursday, the president is scheduled to be in Shenzhen, China, for planned nuclear talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin, plus a separate meeting with Xi.

Now to news from around the country.

The Senate voted 70 to 27 on Monday to advance a bill to speed up permits for energy, mining and power transmission projects. The bill, led by Republican Senator Shelley Moore Capito of West Virginia, would limit some legal challenges under federal environmental laws and aims to cut build times for transmission lines roughly in half. President Trump said, quote, we're behind it 100 percent. Democratic Senator Martin Heinrich of New Mexico said it will help lower energy costs. Democratic Senator Jeff Merkley of Oregon opposes it, saying it speeds the burning of fossil fuels. Kentucky Republican Congressman Brett Guthrie, who chairs the House Energy and Commerce Committee, has said he wants changes on grid reliability. A final Senate vote is expected before the Thanksgiving recess, and then the bill goes to the House.

Air travel is back to normal after Friday's nationwide ground stop. The Federal Aviation Administration says about 12,000 of 50,000 scheduled flights were not completed. Officials said the preliminary cause was a router replacement by a contractor that overloaded the network, and that there are no signs of a cyberattack. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the 12.5 billion dollars already approved for air traffic control upgrades is not enough, and President Trump urged Congress to add funding. Democratic Representatives Rick Larsen and Andre Carson said the administration waited hours to share information and has spent only about 1 billion dollars of the money approved.

Stocks fell sharply on Monday. The Dow dropped more than 700 points, and the Nasdaq fell about two and a half percent. Reuters pointed to uncertainty at Nvidia following the departure of chief executive Jensen Huang, last week's higher-than-expected inflation data, and a new Commerce Department rule requiring licenses for overseas data centers that supply significant computing power to companies based in China, Russia or Iran. Nvidia reports earnings today, and minutes from the Federal Reserve's last meeting come out Wednesday.

California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI on Monday. The lawsuit alleges the company lost control of autonomous artificial intelligence agents more than 80 times in testing, including an incident in which test agents allegedly breached systems at the company Hugging Face. The state is seeking penalties and an injunction. An OpenAI spokesperson said the suit appears to single out the company. The allegations have not been proven in court.

And California is bracing for back-to-back storms called atmospheric rivers. The stronger one arrives late today and lasts through Thursday, with up to 5 inches of rain in some coastal mountains and one to two feet of snow in the Sierra Nevada.

Turning to Kentucky.

Republican Congressman James Comer of Kentucky's 1st District announced Monday that he is running for governor in 2027. He made the announcement in a virtual town hall, less than two weeks after winning a sixth term in Congress. Comer chairs the House Oversight Committee. In 2015, he lost the Republican primary for governor to Matt Bevin by fewer than 100 votes. Comer said eliminating the state income tax is his number one priority, and he said data center companies should pay their own way, calling for an end to large state tax incentives for them. On the Democratic side, Lieutenant Governor Jacqueline Coleman and former state Representative Rocky Adkins are already running. Kentucky Democratic Party Chair Colmon Elridge criticized Comer's record in Congress. Governor Andy Beshear is term-limited.

Lexington had a small earthquake early Monday. The U.S. Geological Survey recorded a magnitude 2.3 quake at 3:07 a.m. near Richmond Road and Man o' War Boulevard. The Herald-Leader reports it is the first quake in the agency's records centered in Lexington. No damage or injuries were reported.

An investigation by the Kentucky Center for Investigative Reporting found that 85 Kentucky law enforcement agencies did not file required reports on cash seized through asset forfeiture last fiscal year. The 289 agencies that did report seized more than 10.1 million dollars, the highest total since 2022. Louisville Metro Police reported more than 2.7 million dollars.

As for weather, Louisville starts with frost and patchy fog this morning, then sunshine, with a high near 60. Highs reach the mid to upper 60s Wednesday through Friday and near 70 on Saturday.

Now, Louisville news.

Louisville Metro Council will keep the same partisan balance next year: 13 Democrats, 12 Republicans and one independent. Council President Brent Ackerson says he will not seek another term as president. The next president needs 14 votes, meaning the winner will need support beyond the Democratic caucus. That vote usually happens early in the new year.

Council Member Ben Reno-Weber is sponsoring a resolution to put a parks property tax on the November 2027 ballot. The tax would be 2.25 cents per 100 dollars of assessed value, about a dollar a week for the average homeowner. Supporters estimate it would raise about 18 million dollars a year for park capital projects. Republicans on the council have signaled opposition. A committee could begin discussing it this afternoon.

Mayor Craig Greenberg has a news conference at 10 this morning with a U.S. Department of Transportation official to announce what his office calls new technological measures for the low-clearance railroad bridge at Third Street and Eastern Parkway, known as the Can Opener. More than 40 trucks were reported stuck there during the I-65 closure this summer.

The Louisville Zoo has its first case of bird flu in its bird collection. A 28-year-old white stork named Awan died November 11 and tested positive. Her mate, Cain, was euthanized Sunday, and his results are pending. The zoo has closed several bird exhibits and moved some birds indoors.

Police have charged a 28-year-old man in the October 26 crash on West Broadway that involved a JCPS school bus. Police say he fled a traffic stop, ran a red light at 18th and Broadway, and hit a car and the bus. Seven people were hospitalized, including two children on the bus. He faces charges including 13 counts of wanton endangerment and is held on a 250,000 dollar bond.

Now, the Bengals.

Cincinnati put on a show on Sunday Night Football. Bengals 38, Steelers 18, at Paycor Stadium, with the team in its White Bengal uniforms. It was 24 to 3 at halftime. Pittsburgh had just 40 yards of offense at the break.

Joe Burrow was 35 of 44 for 409 yards and four touchdowns. According to Bengals.com, Burrow is just the second quarterback in NFL history with four or more touchdown passes in five straight games, joining Peyton Manning in 2004. He now has 27 touchdown passes this season, the most in the league.

Ja'Marr Chase caught 11 passes for 231 yards, the most receiving yards by any player in a game this season. Geoff Hobson of Bengals.com notes it was Chase's fourth career 200-yard game. One more would tie Calvin Johnson and Lance Alworth for the most in NFL history. Chase is up to 878 receiving yards, fourth in the league.

Chase Brown ran for 44 yards and caught eight passes for 39 yards and two touchdowns. Drew Sample caught a 3-yard touchdown, Dohnte Meyers caught a 15-yarder for his first NFL touchdown, and Samaje Perine ran one in from a yard out. The Bengals outgained Pittsburgh 484 yards to 222. Cincinnati has now scored 30 or more points in five straight games, a team record.

Evan McPherson made a 24-yard field goal for a team-record 25th straight make, then missed wide right from 43 yards, his first miss of the season.

On defense, Boye Mafe had his team-leading sixth and a half sack, and Jonathan Allen and Myles Murphy also got to the quarterback. Aaron Rodgers started 0 for 8 and finished 15 of 29 for 143 yards, with both of his touchdown passes coming in the fourth quarter.

Burrow kept it in perspective. Quote: It's one of the biggest wins we've had here in a while, but it's Week 10. He also praised his offensive line, saying, those guys are lights out. Head coach Zac Taylor spoke to the line's play too. Quote: They've been showing that. Maybe people didn't want to see it because they wanted to follow the narrative.

Now, injuries. Right guard Dalton Risner left after 32 snaps with a knee issue. Taylor said Sunday he didn't think it was too serious. On Monday, Taylor said both of Risner's knees started to bother him, and that Risner had also been sick before the game. The team will monitor him. Jalen Rivers played Risner's final 34 snaps and would be the likely fill-in. Guard Dylan Fairchild is also being monitored, according to Russ Heltman of Sports Illustrated. Tee Higgins was seen limping late, but Taylor said he came out of the game healthy. Safety Kyle Dugger missed another game with a quad injury. On the plus side, receiver Andrei Iosivas, cornerback Jalen Davis and guard Brian Parker the Second were cleared to practice last week and could return. No roster moves have been announced since the game.

The Bengals have won five straight and are 7 and 2, first in the AFC North. Pittsburgh is 6 and 3, Cleveland 6 and 4, and Baltimore 4 and 6 after Monday's loss. In the AFC playoff picture as of Monday, Buffalo holds the top seed at 8 and 1. Kansas City and Jacksonville are both 7 and 2, and Cincinnati sits fourth as the division leader, ahead of 7 and 2 Denver. Pittsburgh and Cleveland hold the last two wild-card spots.

Next up, the Bengals visit the Washington Commanders on Monday Night Football, next Monday, November 23, at 8:15 p.m. Eastern. Washington is 3, 5 and 1. Taylor said Monday, quote, we're going to have a tough test on the road next week, against a unique style defense, so we're going to have to go earn it again next week.

Now, the Buffalo Bills.

Bills 31, Jets 12, on Sunday at MetLife Stadium. Buffalo is 8 and 1, its first 8 and 1 start since 1991, and 5 and 0 on the road. Josh Allen threw for 196 yards with a 45-yard touchdown to D.J. Moore and an interception, and he ran for two 1-yard touchdowns. His 325 career touchdowns tie Peyton Manning for the most through a player's first 10 seasons. James Cook ran for 157 yards, including a 74-yard touchdown. Brady called it the best win of the season. Allen said the offense, quote, didn't play too well.

On injuries, cornerback Christian Benford left with a groin injury and defensive back Dee Alford with a concussion. Linebacker Dorian Williams left with a calf injury. The Buffalo News reported linebackers Terrel Bernard and Joe Andreessen had X-rays after the game. Buffalo hosts Miami, which is 1 and 8, this Sunday at 1 p.m. Eastern on CBS.

Now, the Cincinnati Bearcats.

Football lost its third straight game, Iowa State 41, Cincinnati 13, on Saturday in Ames. Cole Tabb ran for 165 yards, his fifth 100-yard game, and is 23 yards short of 1,000 for the season. Coach Scott Satterfield said the third-down conversion rate was the team's worst of the year. The Bearcats are 5 and 5 and host Colorado on Saturday at noon Eastern on ESPN Plus in their home finale at Nippert Stadium.

In men's basketball, Cincinnati won at Dayton 95 to 72 on Saturday to improve to 3 and 1. The Bearcats host Youngstown State Wednesday at 7:30 p.m. Eastern on ESPN Plus.

Now, the Louisville Cardinals.

Football won its fifth straight, Louisville 16, North Carolina 14, Saturday in Chapel Hill. After North Carolina pulled within two with 3 minutes and 43 seconds left, the Cardinals intercepted a pass and ran out the clock. A brawl broke out after the final whistle. No discipline has been announced. Louisville is 7 and 3, 6 and 2 in the ACC, and re-entered the AP poll at number 21. The Cardinals host Pittsburgh on Saturday. Kickoff time and TV have not been announced.

In men's basketball, Memphis beat Louisville 98 to 78 on Sunday after the Cardinals led by 18. Louisville is 2 and 1 and fell from 10th to 23rd in the AP poll. The Cardinals host Stetson Thursday at 8 p.m. Eastern on ACC Network.

Now, the Kentucky Wildcats.

Football lost at home to Florida 21 to 14 on Saturday, the first back-to-back losses of the season. Kenny Minchey threw a touchdown pass to C.J. Baxter and ran for another score, but lost a fumble early in the third quarter that set up a Florida touchdown. Coach Will Stein said, quote, we let a team come in here and physically beat us in all three phases. Kentucky is 7 and 3 and dropped to 17th in the AP poll. The Wildcats play at Missouri Saturday at 7:30 p.m. Eastern on SEC Network.

In men's basketball, Kentucky beat Grambling State 88 to 59 Monday night. Malachi Moreno had career highs of 22 points and 12 rebounds, and Zoom Diallo had 12 points, nine rebounds and nine assists. Kentucky is 4 and 1 but dropped out of the AP Top 25 this week. Mark Pope's team plays Indiana in Indianapolis on Friday at 8 p.m. Eastern on CBS.

And finally, some good news.

In south Louisville, Alaina Norwood and her three children moved into a fully furnished home after four months of homelessness. Furniture Fair furnished it, working with Happy Homes Furniture Bank. Her 8-year-old son, Asher, had asked for a kitchen table, because the family had been eating on the floor.

Louisville case manager Dustin Collins built a free website, No One Left Outside dot org. Five quick questions point Kentucky and Indiana residents to food, housing, utility and mental health help. It has had more than 340,000 unique visits.
