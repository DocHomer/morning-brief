Turning to world news.

Russia launched one of its largest attacks of the war on Ukraine overnight into Friday. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia fired more than 400 drones and more than 110 missiles, including 27 ballistic missiles. The AP reports at least eight civilians were killed, six of them in Kyiv. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said a ballistic missile damaged the Podilskyi Bridge over the Dnipro River, and two other bridges were hit. Russia's Defense Ministry said it hit military-linked targets, and Vladimir Putin had vowed to respond to Ukraine's attacks on the bridge to Crimea. Separately, Poland's Defense Ministry says a Russian cruise missile came down inside Poland, about 37 miles from the Ukrainian border. No one was hurt. Prime Minister Donald Tusk said, quote, we are dealing with an intentional provocation. NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte called it a grave violation of Polish airspace and is expected to visit Poland this weekend. Moscow had no immediate comment.

On the diplomatic side, U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner have proposed discussing Europe's security after the war. The AP reports the Kremlin is using that opening to restate demands it made in December 2021, including no NATO expansion to Ukraine or Georgia. Kremlin adviser Yuri Ushakov said Putin will send the envoys, quote, fresh considerations. Putin said it makes no sense to discuss European security without the Europeans and invited a European delegation to Moscow, but he also said any European troops in postwar Ukraine would be legitimate targets. No date or format for talks has been set.

In the Middle East, President Trump said at his news conference that Iran wants a deal, but, quote, they don't want to make a deal that's acceptable to me. CBS News reports, citing sources, that the U.S. and Israel are weighing a new assault in the short term. Iran's Foreign Ministry rejected the president's claim this week of an indication that Iran sought chemical or biological weapons, calling it, quote, the same fraudulent script used against Iraq in 2003. The Saudi-led coalition says it intercepted Houthi missiles and drones aimed at Jazan on Friday and three ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh overnight.

At the United Nations climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, nearly 200 countries went into overtime without a deal. The summit, called COP31, was set to end Friday. Monday's first draft called for a faster phase-out of fossil fuels, but Thursday's draft removed every mention of them after Saudi Arabia and other oil producers objected. Summit President Murat Kurum said, quote, we are close to the finish line, but we are not there yet. The European Union said the draft risks turning the summit into a, quote, fossil summit, and Panama has threatened to walk out. A new draft is expected today. The United States is not taking part for a second year.

Denmark accused Russia of plotting to kill a Ukrainian-American rapper at a concert north of Copenhagen in September. Foreign Minister Lars Lokke Rasmussen called it intimidation meant to make Denmark waver in its support for Ukraine, and gave Russia's defense attache seven days to leave. Russia's embassy called the allegations unsubstantiated.

Now to news from around the country.

Wall Street finished its worst week in years on Friday. The Dow fell 950 points, or 1.9 percent, to 48,147. The S and P 500 fell 3.7 percent and the Nasdaq dropped 5.3 percent. For the week, the S and P lost 8.6 percent, the Dow 8 percent, and the Nasdaq 10 percent. Fox Business reports the Dow and S and P are now in a correction, meaning 10 percent or more below their records, and it was the S and P's seventh straight loss, its longest losing streak since February 2020. The Nasdaq is now down for the year. Much of the drop came from Nvidia, which fell 14 percent after Reuters reported, citing two people familiar with the matter, that Securities and Exchange Commission enforcement staff are reviewing potential accounting issues at the company. The S E C declined to comment, and the review is not a finding of wrongdoing. Broadcom fell about 12 percent on a report that Google is cutting orders for its A I chips.

President Trump held a news conference of about two hours at the White House on Friday, joined by Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio. He called his Asia trip one of the most successful presidential visits to Asia in American history, and said his meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin was primarily about Ukraine. Asked whether Putin is a friend to America, he said, quote, I can't speak to America. Rubio said it would be dangerous if leaders could not engage. On Republican losses in the midterms, the president said, quote, I wasn't on the ballot, I hope you remember that. Vance had written after the election that, quote, the reason we lost is the war. The president called that accurate, and said he would absolutely go to war with Iran again, because, quote, we cannot allow Iran to have a nuclear weapon. He said he would veto bills from the Democratic-led House unless Democrats work with him, and named health care and college sports as possible common ground.

In Congress, the fight over government funding is centering on the Iran war. Funding runs out December 11, and lawmakers left for Thanksgiving without a deal. They return November 30. Senate Majority Leader John Thune said, quote, the American people don't need another government shutdown, and on Friday said there were no deals yet. Republicans accuse Democrats of stalling ahead of the December 1 vote for Senate Democratic leader between Chuck Schumer and Chris Van Hollen. Some Democrats say any short-term funding bill must restrict war spending. Senator Kirsten Gillibrand said, quote, we do not want to fund this war. Other Democrats oppose a shutdown. Representative Rosa DeLauro said, quote, shutting the government down doesn't stop the war.

In Florida, the St. Johns River at Cocoa broke a record set in 1953, reaching 18.7 feet Friday night, well above major flood stage. The Weather Service expects it to stay near that level through early next week, and the flooding is moving north along the river. State wildlife officers report about 120 rescues in Brevard County and more than 150 in Volusia County, which declared an emergency. No deaths have been reported from this week's flooding.

And in Los Angeles, a federal jury convicted Jonathan Rinderknecht of starting the Palisades Fire, which killed 12 people in January 2025. U.S. Attorney Bill Essayli praised his team's work. Defense attorney Steve Haney said the defense was disappointed and will review appeal options. Sentencing is set for March 17. Each count carries 5 to 20 years.

Turning to Kentucky.

Governor Andy Beshear and Republican legislative leaders are at odds over whether to reopen the state's two-year budget in January. Revised forecasts project nearly 1 billion dollars in extra revenue this fiscal year, and Beshear has used some of the unallocated money to fill a Medicaid gap, fund Pre-K for All, add money for senior meals and pay to forgive 250 million dollars in medical debt. House Speaker David Osborne, a Prospect Republican, said the governor's moves had, quote, invaded state funds, and said, quote, I don't think we have any choice. Beshear said that if Republicans take away Medicaid coverage, quote, good luck in your 2027 elections. Lawmakers are expected to take up the budget in the 30-day session that starts in January.

State health and education officials have alerted school districts about Kentucky's measles outbreaks. As of November 4, the state had 204 cases this year across 14 counties, with 13 people hospitalized. The largest outbreaks are in Christian and Laurel counties, with 66 cases each, and Trigg County with 32. All but two cases were in people who were unvaccinated or whose status was unknown. Deputy Commissioner Lori Caloia said the rise is, quote, primarily driven by increased vaccine hesitancy. Schools are being asked to review response plans and vaccine requirements and remind families to keep sick children home.

A Louisville man who ran for office because of a bill aimed at immigrants has won a seat in the Kentucky House. Democrat Suhas Kulkarni beat Republican incumbent Ken Fleming in the 48th District by 811 votes. He decided to run in January, after a bill was filed to bar people not born in the U.S. from holding elected office in Kentucky. He called that bill, quote, a slap in the face of what the founding fathers felt America should be. Kulkarni came to Louisville from India 40 years ago and is a University of Louisville professor. His daughter, Representative Nima Kulkarni, represents the 40th District, making them the first father and daughter to serve together in the Kentucky House.

The Jefferson County Teachers Association is pushing JCPS to make teacher pay a priority in the next budget. Union President Maddie Shephard said, quote, teachers can't afford to work in JCPS, and said teachers' buying power has fallen more than 20 percent since 2011. The lowest-paid full-time JCPS teacher makes about 42,600 dollars, while Fayette and Oldham counties start teachers above 50,000. JCPS has cut its projected deficit from 188 million dollars last year to about 51 and a half million, and the district said it's committed to competitive pay while it works on a recovery plan. Negotiations begin Monday.

The private company that provided backup ambulance service in Louisville is disputing the city's decision not to renew its contract. AmeriPro Health's Brian Bishop told WDRB the company got 36 hours' notice, estimates it saved the city about 2 million dollars a year, and says suburban fire departments' call volume is up about 200 percent since. Under a 2024 state law, the city pays suburban fire districts for those runs. The city says it has added staff and vehicles, with more increases expected in the coming weeks.

And a claim from the world of math. TechCrunch reports Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpoge says he has a computer-checkable proof of the Riemann hypothesis, a famous unsolved problem from 1859, developed with help from Anthropic's Claude. The proof is written in a language called Lean, which lets a computer verify each logical step. Several members of the Lean community, including M I T's Andrew Sutherland, say it checks out by computer, but mathematicians have not yet fully understood it, and Alpoge has promised a human-readable explanation. And finally, some good news.

UofL Health is launching Kentucky's first mobile lung cancer screening unit. Kentucky has the highest rate of new lung cancer cases in the country, about 84 per 100,000 people compared with 52 nationally, and only about 20 percent of eligible people get screened. The 1.5 million dollar unit will travel to rural and underserved counties within 90 miles, starting in mid-2027. Laura Jones, who directs screening at the Brown Cancer Center, said, quote, there might not even be a lung cancer screening unit in some of these counties.

NASA's Voyager 1 has become the first spacecraft ever to reach one light-day from Earth, about 16 billion miles. That means a radio signal takes a full day to make the trip. Voyager 1 launched in 1977 on a five-year mission, and turns 50 next year. It stopped sending science data last week but still sends a strong signal, and project manager Suzanne Dodd said, quote, I'm confident that it won't be too long, and it will send us data back.

And in Prairie Village, Kansas, a teenager named Sonny Gallardo started a landscaping business in sixth grade, raking leaves door to door. Four years later, thieves stole his trailer and all five of his mowers, about 20,000 dollars of equipment, before his insurance was in place. His community raised 37,000 dollars to replace it.
