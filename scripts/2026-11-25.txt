Turning to world news.

Drones struck a tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District, injuring 11 people, according to Saudi Civil Defense. Several outlets reported the building houses the headquarters of the Public Investment Fund, Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund. It is the second strike on the district in less than a week. Saudi-led coalition spokesman Major General Turki al-Maliki promised a, quote, firm response, and the Gulf Cooperation Council condemned the attack. Separately, Saudi air defenses said they intercepted four drones over Ras Tanura in the Eastern Province this morning, with no injuries. The Houthis said they targeted the refinery there. There was no independent confirmation of any damage.

In the war in Ukraine, Ukrainian drones reportedly hit the Syzran and Kuibyshev oil refineries in Russia's Samara region overnight, starting large fires. Russian President Vladimir Putin said retaliation is, quote, inevitable, and accused Kyiv of continuing attacks despite a request from President Trump. In southern Ukraine, regional governor Mykhailo Semikin said a Russian overnight attack on the city of Zaporizhzhia killed two people and wounded about 40, including four children.

Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said Iran would use, quote, all its tools against the United States naval blockade, which he called a military aggression. The blockade was reinstated in July. Iranian state media also reported that Iran's Revolutionary Guard Navy said two oil tankers struck naval mines and caught fire after taking what it called an unauthorized route through the Strait of Hormuz. That report has not been independently verified.

Venezuela's finance ministry on Tuesday published its first updated economic data in years, disclosing 237.5 billion dollars in public debt. According to the Financial Times and Bloomberg, that includes about 110 billion dollars in defaulted bonds and unpaid interest, and more than 26 billion dollars in arbitration awards. The figures put the debt at about 242 percent of the country's economic output, ahead of what is expected to be one of the largest government debt restructurings ever.

China launched its Shenzhou-24 spacecraft this morning from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center. The crew includes Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, who becomes Pakistan's first astronaut to reach orbit and the first foreign crew member aboard China's Tiangong space station. He is expected to spend about a week there. Also in China, the ruling party's anti-corruption agency opened an investigation into Shanghai party chief Chen Jining, a member of the Politburo.

And Malaysia says it will send 6,803 migrants and refugees back to Myanmar in December, some by navy ship and most by air. Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said Malaysia can no longer keep hosting them and that efforts to resettle them in other countries failed. The United Nations has urged Malaysia to halt the returns.

Now, news from around the country.

A federal jury in Brooklyn found the Chinese technology company Huawei guilty on 10 of 12 counts, including bank fraud, wire fraud, sanctions violations and conspiracy to steal trade secrets. Prosecutors said the company misled international banks about its business in Iran. Jurors acquitted Huawei of racketeering conspiracy and conspiracy to obstruct justice. Sentencing is set for September 2027. Huawei said the verdict is without legal or factual merit, and its lead lawyer, Brian Heberlig, said the company expects to appeal.

The Army says it will go ahead this morning with the detonation of degrading explosives at a facility in southeastern Iowa. Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle called the decision the, quote, unanimous judgement of federal agencies. A federal court rejected an emergency request from environmental groups to stop it, and an appeals court approved the plan. On Tuesday the Army expanded the evacuation zone and urged children and pregnant women to leave the area. President Trump said Iowa Governor-elect Rob Sand, a Democrat, should either support the detonation or be held responsible for any damage.

President Trump used a speech at the Justice Department this week to criticize California Governor Gavin Newsom for commuting more than 500 death sentences, calling it, quote, one of the most shocking acts of injustice our country has ever seen. The Wall Street Journal reported the president plans a directive telling federal prosecutors to speed up federal death sentences and seek the death penalty in more cases. Newsom said the decision was among the hardest of his career, and said in a statement, quote, I won't take lectures on accountability from a President who has used the pardon power to help wealthy friends. California's Supreme Court must approve most of the commutations.

The Wall Street Journal and Politico report the administration is preparing to raise tariffs on China, the European Union and 14 other trading partners after an investigation into industrial overcapacity. China could face duties of up to 20 percent, and the European Union, Japan and South Korea rates of 15 percent or less. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer has argued that foreign government subsidies let overseas manufacturers undercut American producers. Nothing has been formally announced. The plan would rebuild tariffs after the Supreme Court struck down the original levies in February, and legal challenges are expected.

California Democratic Congressman Jimmy Gomez says he will resign next week. The House Ethics Committee said it found, quote, substantial evidence of unwelcome sexual conduct toward employees, including some behavior it said may qualify as a criminal misdemeanor.

And if you're traveling for the holiday, the Federal Aviation Administration expects its busiest Thanksgiving travel period in 16 years. At Tuesday's White House turkey pardon, President Trump warned travelers to expect slow air traffic.

Here at home, Kentucky news.

Governor Andy Beshear has created a Kentucky Office of Outdoor Recreation. He signed the executive order Tuesday at Cove Spring Park in Frankfort. The office will oversee the Kentucky Trail Town Program, which includes 32 communities, and work to grow outdoor tourism. Seth Wheat, who worked in interpretation and engagement for Kentucky State Parks, was named executive director. Beshear said, quote, our record-breaking 14.6 billion dollar tourism industry is essential to our commonwealth's success. The state says outdoor recreation brings about 8.7 billion dollars in economic impact. A new outdoor recreation license plate is also available online.

The Kentucky Lantern looks at the legislature after this month's election, when nine Republican incumbents lost their seats, mostly in suburban districts around Louisville, including Senators Julie Raque Adams of Louisville and Lindsey Tichenor of Smithfield. Republicans still hold supermajorities in both chambers. Senate Republican Floor Leader Max Wise said he does not think state policy directly affected those races, and said, quote, I don't think you're going to see us take our foot off the gas as a supermajority. House Minority Floor Leader Lindsey Burke, a Lexington Democrat, said she is hopeful there will be some shifts in the majority party's priorities. Issues expected in 2027 include housing, data center electric rates, and limits on license plate reader cameras.

Also Tuesday, WKYT reported the number of confirmed measles cases in Kentucky has reached 229, with the newest cases in Christian and Trigg counties. FEMA announced more than 38 million dollars approved for Kentucky. And Judge Phillip Shepherd ruled that challenges to Kentucky's lethal injection procedures can move forward, in a lawsuit brought by death row inmate Ralph Baze.

Now to Louisville.

A small plane crash-landed on Emerson Avenue in the Strathmore Village area Tuesday afternoon, between Taylorsville and Bardstown roads and less than a mile from Bowman Field. Fire officials said two people were taken to the hospital, one with life-threatening injuries and one with serious injuries. Air traffic audio shows the pilot reported engine trouble, saying, quote, I'm not going to make the field, and later, I'm going into the houses, I'll try to avoid whatever I can. No homes were damaged, though some trees came down. A dog on board is in the care of Louisville Metro Animal Services. The plane was a single-engine Beech Bonanza.

A WLKY investigation found more than 20 emails and memos raising concerns about the private ambulance company AmeriPro Health before Louisville Metro ended its partnership with the company. The records allege missed dispatch calls, delayed responses and equipment failures. Emergency management director Jody Meiman wrote to the company about a month into the contract, quote, this is not the expectation for level of care that we signed the contract for. Mayor Craig Greenberg said the allegations played a role in the decision. An AmeriPro official, Brian Bishop, disputed the allegations, saying the company made more than 10,000 responses with 11 concerns raised. Greenberg said the city will continue to staff up its own EMS service.

Police say a woman was shot and wounded inside the Walmart in the 10400 block of Dixie Highway just before 6 p.m. Tuesday, near the checkout area. Her injuries are not considered life-threatening. Two people were detained, and police say those involved knew each other.

And with tonight being one of the busiest bar nights of the year, Louisville police are warning against drunk driving over the holiday.

Turning to artificial intelligence.

Amazon has agreed to acquire the technology behind Decart's AI optimization software, along with Decart's founding team and researchers. Bloomberg reported the deal is worth 7.5 billion dollars. The software helps AI models run more efficiently on chips from Nvidia, Amazon and Google. Amazon is preparing to sell its own AI chips, called Trainium, to outside customers. Peter DeSantis, who leads custom chips at Amazon, said, quote, every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip.

The attorneys general of Oregon and Virginia have filed separate lawsuits against SpaceX, which merged with Elon Musk's AI company xAI, over the Grok chatbot's image tools. The suits allege consumer protection violations, saying Grok was used to create sexualized images of real people without their consent, including material involving children, and that the companies promoted the product as safe. Virginia's complaint says the weak safeguards were, quote, not a failure but a feature. X's safety team said the people who made the images deliberately broke its rules, and that it screens prompts and results with automated filters. The states are seeking penalties and court orders.

Anthropic, the maker of the Claude chatbot, released a set of tools for software teams called Claude Ship. Anthropic says the AI agents, meaning programs that carry out tasks on their own, can fix failed software checks, respond to review comments, and approve routine code changes according to a team's written policy. It is rolling out to business customers who use GitHub. Separately, OpenAI published research claiming its GPT-6 models designed new proteins using general reasoning rather than biology-specific software. OpenAI says about 11 percent of the small proteins it designed bound to their targets in lab tests. The results have not been independently reviewed.

In health care, Britain's National Health Service has started a 10 million pound trial called EDITH, testing whether AI systems can let one specialist read mammograms that two specialists review today. Invitations have begun going out, and the trial plans to include nearly 700,000 women at 30 sites over three years.

And following Nvidia's decision to bring back Jensen Huang as chief executive, Yale's Jeffrey Sonnenfeld told TechCrunch the move creates a, quote, historic level of individual liability for the board. Five shareholder lawsuits have been filed, and Huang is due to testify before Congress next week.

Now, the Bengals.

Cincinnati is 7 and 3 after Monday night's 22 to 20 loss at Washington, which ended a five-game winning streak. Washington's Drew Stevens kicked a 26-yard field goal as time expired. The Bengals led 10 to 6 at halftime on an Evan McPherson field goal and a 31-yard touchdown catch by Tee Higgins, his seventh of the season. In the third quarter, Joe Burrow rolled out and found rookie Colbie Young for a 4-yard score, Young's first career touchdown, to make it 17 to 10. The Bengals never trailed until Washington's field goal with under three minutes left. McPherson answered from 56 yards with 46 seconds to go to put Cincinnati back in front, 20 to 19, but Jayden Daniels hit Antonio Williams on two deep passes to set up the winning kick. Daniels had 146 passing yards in the fourth quarter alone. The game was also halted for about four minutes in the third quarter when the stadium lights went out.

The talk since has focused on the Bengals' final offensive drive. Out of the two-minute warning, Burrow hit Mike Gesicki for 32 yards to about the Washington 33. Rather than run the ball and play for a field goal, the Bengals threw on third down, and Burrow was sacked. Zac Taylor explained the thinking. Quote: Trying to get a first down to win it. That way, the walk-off field goal would win the game for us. He added, quote, you run it twice, then you're disappointed that you're in fourth-and-nine and you're kicking a field goal. Burrow took responsibility for the sack. Quote: If we get the first down, we don't give them a chance to have the ball. I made a really bad play. He also said, quote, 40 seconds is plenty of time to go down and get into field goal range.

Burrow finished 28 of 40 for 300 yards, two touchdowns and no interceptions, and he was sacked three times. According to the team, he leads the NFL with 3,095 passing yards through 11 weeks. Gesicki had five catches for 77 yards, and Samaje Perine had 97 yards from scrimmage, 71 of them receiving. Washington held Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins to a combined 74 yards. On defense, Boye Mafe had a career-high three sacks, all in the first half, giving him nine and a half on the season, the team lead. Dexter Lawrence had a career-high 10 tackles for loss. Lawrence said afterward, quote, they beat us. We've got to own that as a team. Chase said, quote, one loss doesn't determine nothing. And Higgins said, quote, when you win some, you lose some, you just gotta live to see the next day.

On injuries, cornerback Jalen Davis and receiver Andrei Iosivas missed Monday's game, and Sports Illustrated lists both on injured reserve with a designation to return. Guard Dalton Risner, who sat out with a knee issue, and safety Kyle Dugger are listed as questionable for this week, and linebacker Oren Burks is on injured reserve with a groin injury. The first official practice report of the week has not been released.

In the standings, the Bengals still lead the AFC North, one game ahead of the 6 and 4 Cleveland Browns. Sports Illustrated notes Burrow slipped to second behind Brock Purdy in the betting odds for Most Valuable Player after Monday's game.

Next up, the Bengals host the New Orleans Saints, who are 3 and 7, on Sunday at 1 p.m. Eastern at Paycor Stadium, on CBS. DraftKings has Cincinnati as a 10-point favorite. The Saints list offensive tackle Asim Richards as doubtful, with center Erik McCoy, linebacker Pete Werner, cornerback Martin Emerson Junior and tackle Talliese Fuaga questionable. After that, the Bengals visit Cleveland on December 6th.

Finally, some good news.

Here in Louisville, the Bikes or Bust drive has wrapped up for the year, with volunteers collecting more than 2,200 bikes for children in Louisville and southern Indiana.

In Utah, a man named Rob Adams is keeping a promise he made to his mother as a child. His family once lived out of a pickup truck in Texas, and when he was 11, a neighboring family took them in for Thanksgiving. Adams said, quote, I feel like I owe this to another family because someone was so kind to me. His group, Thanksgiving Heroes, aimed to feed 10 families its first year and ended up serving about 755. This year it plans to deliver meal kits to 3,600 Utah families, and its affiliates have served more than 29,000 families nationwide.

In Brazil, researchers report that releasing mosquitoes carrying a natural bacteria called Wolbachia, which blocks the viruses mosquitoes spread, cut dengue infections among children by 68 percent in a four-year trial in the city of Belo Horizonte. It happened during Brazil's largest dengue outbreak on record, and chikungunya infections dropped 73 percent. Johns Hopkins researcher Derek Cummings, who helped lead the trial, said the approach, quote, substantially reduced dengue infections.

And on Prince Edward Island in Canada, retired math teacher Rob Burke turned donated broken guitars, a violin and drum parts into bird feeders. He calls it the Peck and Play Orchestra, and woodpeckers, chickadees and blue jays have all stopped by for a meal.
